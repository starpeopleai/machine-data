WAS IT VENUS?

On the morning hours of August 17, several law enforcement
Officers as well as civilians of the lake-bordering counties, east of
Cleveland, reported strange celestial phenomena.

Radio icati carried the word, and small aggrega-
tions of officers gathered at different locations toobserve the strange
light or lights in the eastern sky.

A. E. Candusso, of FSIC, and Gary Davis, of M US, of Me-
dina, were on the scene the same night of the sightings (Monday,
August 17).

Ptl. George Miller of Mentor-on-Lake, and Ron Deitrick, guard
at the Yacht Club docks showed us the viewing spot. A co-observer,
(3 in all here) Councilman Robert Tailor, of Mentor-on-Lake was
not present.

The very brilliant, white object was seen in the eastern sky, it
was the only bright object in the clear, crisp sky; the rest being
ordinary stars. The object seemed to dance in place, diminish in
size and return to brilliance. At one time it disappeared completely
for 5-10 minutes. They said the object remained stationary, or
Seemed to be a little higher and to the right. As the sun rose, the
Stars disappeared, but this object remained visible for sometime be-
fore being overcome by the sun's light.

Summation: The time, direction, duration, description coin-
cides with the rise of the planet Venus, which rises about 3 hours
before the sun. The unusual effects — probably unusual atmospheric
conditions.

(Remaining sighters interviewed by Editor).

Dep. Sheriff Michael Genco, of Lake County joined two other
officers on the bridge spanning I.S. 90 and S.R. 306. The brilliant
white light was as large as a cup (apparent size). It was just N

getting much smaller, then returning to its original brilliance. In

halo was close to the central object. On two occasions there seemed
to be a red flame or flash coming out of the bottom right area.
