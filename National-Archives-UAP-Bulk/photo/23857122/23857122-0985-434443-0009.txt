"FLYING SAUCERi
At approximately 2050 hours on 2 April 1952, It. Robert Baldwin of
the 727th AC& Squadron telephoned the Chief Duty Controller at Exceed
'that he had Just seen a #flying saucer". It. Baldwin was inspecting the
guard at an entrance to the area when he saw an object appear from a
southwesterly direction and pass overhead continuing for some ten (10)
seconds to the ME at which time it disappeared below the horizon.
In It. Baldwin's opinion, at no time did the object leave a trail
such as a meteor gives off. Approximately half way along it's path it
appeared to add speed, which was evidenced by an increase in the trail
of fire, lasting approximately three (3) or four (4) seconds, then re.
verting
to a slight slow.
In his estimation the speed of the object was between 1000/1200 mph.
Immediately after the passage of the object, it was possible to hear it.
The sound was the same as given off by a jet aircraft at high altitude
and speed, The object was witnessed by It. Baldwin, Set, Carey and Pfc.
Barnett.
