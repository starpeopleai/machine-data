At MT. CLEMENS, MICHIGAN on 17 July 1952 at 23318, AIRMAN SECOND
CLASS JAMES E. WEBB observed while looking through the windows of his home,
a light of bluish white radiance moving at right angles to his line of vision
and in a northeasterly direction. (Airman Webb was facing southeast during
sighting.)
2. Due to the fact that the area in which the light seemed to be moving
is frequently used by aircraft making landings at SELFRIDGE AIR FORCE BASE,
A/2C WEBB reasoned that the light was sunlight reflected from the fuselage of
an aircraft. A/2C WEBB afterwards determined that the light could not be re-
flected light since it never sparkled or changed color.
The light was very
brilliant and nearly as bright as an are welder's torch but not as blue,
according to AIRMAN WEBB.
When first observed, the light was traveling at
approximately four hundred (400) miles per hour in a horizontal path.
There
seemed to be no radiation or trail from the light.
3. AIRMAN WEBB states that there was no distinguishable sound.
AIRMAN WEBB states that he watched the object for approximately
eight (8)
seconds but
could not accurately judge the size or
distance of the
light.
A 20 WEBB also states that he is unfamiliar with the area in which
the light was observed but it appeared to be in the vicinity of the southern
end of SELFRIDGE AIR FORCE BASE.
