Sighting of Burning Object in the Air
Rio Linda, California
8 September 1950
At 1930 hours on 8 September 1950, Nr. JOSEPH E. LATTA, civilian, was inter-
viewed by Special Agents THOMAS J. SCANLAN and RICHARD J. OSBORN, at LATTA's
residence on Taylor Street, Del Paso Heights, California. Mr. LATTA stated that
during the day he had been working on his rice farm which is located approximately
twenty-seven miles north of Sacramento, California on the old Marysville Road.
At approximately 1400 hours, he had paused in his work and while obtaining a drink
of water had noted a burning object falling very slowly through the air. LATTA
asserted that when he first noticed the object, it was at an altitude of approxi-
mately two hundred feet, appeared to be about ten feet in diameter, and was falling
at a very slow rate of speed. The light of the burning object was extremely bright
and made it impossible to determine any specific details relative to shape other than
that it appeared round. The object was leaving a trail of blue smoke as it descended.
LATTA stated it struck the ground approximately 1-1/4 miles distant on the near
side of a row of trees bordering a creek. He was of the opinion it had landed in
a rice paddy as there was no evidence of any conflagration after it landed. He
further stated that he could guide Special Agents OSBORN and SCANLAN to within a
thousand feet of the spot where the object landed.
2. On 8 September 1950, a night search of the area indicated by LATTA failed
to reveal any evidence of a burning object having landed in the vicinity. Due to
the fact that part of the area is under water (rice paddies) search was discontinued
until the following morning.
3. On 9 September 1950, Mr. EDGAR ERNST, R #1, Box 3100, Rio Linda, California,
the person on whose property subject object purportedly landed, was interviewed at
his rice farm approximately one and one half miles northeast of LATTA's farm. ERNST
stated that the object seen by LATTA was probably a flare which he had shot to scare
ducks which nested in his rice paddies.
ERNST asserted he had obtained a rifle,
grenade launcher and flares from the U. S. Fish and wildlife Authority to disperse
the ducks and that on 8 September 1950, he had shot flares at approximately 140u
hours, 1600 hours and 1700 hours. ERNST stated that chances of recovering the dis
charged flares were very small due to the fact that he did not know exactly where
they landed and thet the remains were probably under water in the rice paddies.
