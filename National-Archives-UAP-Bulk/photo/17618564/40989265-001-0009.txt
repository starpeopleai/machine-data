With two separate ascents in 1931 and
1932, the Swiss physicist Auguste Piccard
took the lead in the climb upward.
In his
first flight Piccard and an assistant lifted off
from Augsberg, Germany inside an enclos-
ed, pressurized, spherical gondola made of
aluminum. Suspended from a rubberized,
hydrogen filled balloon, they attained the
record altitude of 51,961 feet. On the second
flight, again with an assistant, the old
record was broken, the gondola reaching an
altitude of 53,139 feet.
Not only had Piccard reached into the
stratosphere... where no man had gone
before... but while there, was able to glean
new information on the nature of gamma
rays and cosmic rays as they exit earth's at-
mosphere. Compounding his success was
the unique design and operation of his
balloon; this became his major contribution
to the scientific efforts of his time.
