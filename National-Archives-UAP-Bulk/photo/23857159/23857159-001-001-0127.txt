On 28 October 1954, at 1732 hours, It. Col. Olen C. Cook and 1st Lt. James W.
Brown observed a bright light in the sky South South West of Miho AB.
They
observed it rising slowly, appearing to get brighter and larger. After about
forty-five (45) seconds of observing it, they report that the object made a
ninety (90) degree tur and moved rapidly across a short portion of the sky,
about five (5) degrees azimuth. It then disappeared behind a cloud and did
not reappear.
At one time the object passed between the observers and a cloud.
One observer looked at the object through binoculars for a short time and
described it as being round to oval and giving out pulsating light, possibly
due to rotation.
Both officers are considered to be reliable men and are positive of this
observation, but preparing officer can attach no immediate significance to
incident.
