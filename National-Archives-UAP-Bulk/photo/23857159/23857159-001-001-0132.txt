1. Object Description
a. Size
The object was large, but no attempt was made to guess the size in foot
measurements. All three observers agreed that the object was as long as the di-
ameter of a basketball held at arms length, but about the diameter of an egg held
at arm's length in thickness. The object never changed shape or size.
b. Shape
When sighted by Technical Sergeant Stratton (the man who saw it first),
the object was curved like an arc of a circle, six or seven times longer than
wide and had rounded ends. Sgt Stratton stated that the end pointing toward the
aircraft flight path was lower than the other end.
Major Hardin and Capt Rouch, who were interrogated together but
separately from Sgt Stratton, confirmed the shape of the object except for the
sparks shooting from either end.
e. Color
Sgt Stratton said that the body of the object was blue and the sparks
emanating from each end were deep orange.
The blue color was like a neon light
or the color of the exhaust of a reciprocating engine at cruising mixture.
The
light was bright and lit up the cockpit of the aircraft, but was not bright enough
to hurt the eyes.
Major Hardin and Capt Rouch were not looking in the direction of the
object at the time it first appeared. They were attracted by it after the original
flash lit up the cockpit.
At the time these officers saw the object, their impressions were
that the object was deep orange in color and neither of them could remember seeing
sparks at either end.
They saw the object for about two seconds, while Sgt Stratton said
he saw it for about three seconds.
d. Number
One (1).
e. Formation
None.
f. Features
No external projections or flight surfaces discernible.
g. Trail or Exhaust
Sgt Stratton saw an orderly trail of deep orange sparks coming from
the rear end of the object and a haphazard pattern (like tracer bullets except in
color) emanating from the front end.
Major Hardin and Capt Rouch saw no exhaust or trail and did not see
the sparks.
